Ingredients

  • canned tuna (Steakist)steak-style — check species (see safety note)2 cans (~280 g drained)
  • cottage cheese1 tub (250 g)
  • Shkedi soft white cheese1 tub (250 g)
  • eggs3, beaten
  • breadcrumbs½ cup (~54 g)
  • onion2 medium (~300 g)
  • yellow cheese100 g
  • salt, pepperto taste

🍳 Cooking guide — for best taste & texture

  1. Drain the tuna very well and flake it — excess liquid makes the patties loose.
  2. Grate the onion (or finely chop and squeeze out the juice) and grate the yellow cheese so both spread evenly through the mix.
  3. Mix the tuna, cottage cheese, soft cheese, eggs, breadcrumbs, onion and yellow cheese; season with salt, pepper and a squeeze of lemon plus fresh dill or parsley to lift the fish.
  4. If the mix is too wet to shape, add breadcrumbs a spoon at a time until it holds; chill 15-20 min to firm up.
  5. Form patties and bake at 200 C (400 F) ~20 min, flipping once, until set and golden — baking keeps them lean with no added oil.
  6. Or pan-fry over medium in a little oil for a crispier crust, ~3 min per side.
  7. Rest a few minutes so they firm before serving.

For him  8/10

weight loss · muscle retention · sperm
A strong high-protein dish — 29 g protein for ~283 kcal (~10 g protein per 100 kcal) from tuna + eggs + a stack of cheeses, with good selenium (~45 mcg, a sperm micro from the tuna/eggs), calcium, and a little omega-3. Fat (~12 g) and sodium (~400 mg) come mostly from the yellow cheese + cottage/Shkedi; you can lighten by trimming the yellow cheese. Canned light tuna is fine for you in these modest per-serving amounts (~⅓ can/serving).
Highlights
  • high protein density (~10 g/100 kcal)
  • selenium from tuna + eggs (sperm micro)
  • calcium + some omega-3
  • fat/sodium from the cheeses (yellow cheese is the lever)
  • no cooking oil listed — bake to keep it lean
Suggested modifications
  • Bake rather than fry to keep fat down (no oil was listed anyway)
  • Drop or halve the 100 g yellow cheese — biggest fat/sodium lever with little protein loss
  • Serve over salad/greens to add the fiber + micros the dish is light on

For her  6/10

preconception / prenatal (advisory)
Nutrient-rich for her — protein, calcium, choline (eggs), selenium, iodine, some omega-3 — BUT this is the one dish carrying a real mercury flag: it's tuna. That keeps it a 'fine in moderation, mind the details' rather than a staple. The per-serving tuna load is modest (~⅓ can), which helps.
Highlights
  • protein + calcium + choline (eggs) + selenium + iodine
  • some omega-3 EPA/DHA from the tuna
  • modest per-serving tuna amount (~⅓ can) keeps mercury exposure lower
Safety advisories
  • ⚠ TUNA = mercury. Use LIGHT/skipjack canned tuna, not albacore/white or high-mercury species — 'Steakist' steak-style may be a larger (higher-mercury) species, so check. Once actively trying/pregnant, keep total tuna to ~2 servings/week of light tuna (1/week if albacore). — swap: light/skipjack tuna, or swap to canned salmon/sardines (lower mercury, more omega-3)
  • ⚠ Cheeses must be pasteurized (Israeli cottage/yellow/Shkedi are) — fine. Bake eggs fully. — swap: pasteurized cheeses; cook eggs through
Suggested modifications
  • Swapping the tuna for canned SALMON removes the mercury flag AND raises omega-3 + vitamin D — a strictly better preconception version
  • Confirm the tuna is light/skipjack and count it toward her weekly tuna limit in lihitrack

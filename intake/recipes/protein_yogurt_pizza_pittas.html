🍳 Cooking guide — for best taste & texture

  1. In a bowl, mix the flour, baking powder and salt, then add the yogurt, olive oil and cold water. Stir to a shaggy dough, then knead ~1-2 min just until smooth — don't overwork it (over-kneading makes tough pittas).
  2. Cover and rest the dough 10-15 min — this lets the flour hydrate and the gluten relax, so it rolls out easily and cooks up soft.
  3. Divide into 8-9 balls. On a floured surface, roll each ~½ cm thick (thin enough to cook through, thick enough to stay fluffy).
  4. For the pizza version: spread a little pizza sauce and cheese on one half, fold over (or top a second round) and press the edges to seal.
  5. Heat a pan over medium with a thin film of olive oil. Cook each pitta ~2-3 min per side until deep golden with brown spots and the cheese is melted — medium heat, not high, so the inside cooks before the outside burns.
  6. Keep the finished pittas under a clean towel so they stay soft.
  7. Tip: no yeast means no rise time, but the 10-15 min rest is what keeps them tender — don't skip it.

For him  6/10

weight loss · muscle retention · sperm
A better-than-average bread base: the protein yogurt lifts each pitta to ~5.6 g protein for 157 kcal — more than a plain pitta (~3-4 g) — with a bit of olive-oil fat. But it's refined white flour, so low fiber (~0.8 g) and mostly fast carbs. As a controlled higher-protein carb/bread swap it's fine; it's a vehicle, not a protein source. Watch the FILLING — that's where the cheese piles on calories, saturated fat and sodium (unquantified here).
Highlights
  • higher protein than normal bread (~5.6 g/pitta from the yogurt)
  • refined white flour, low fiber (~0.8 g)
  • moderate sodium (~205 mg/pitta before filling)
  • filling (cheese/sauce) is the real calorie/sodium lever
Suggested modifications
  • Swap half the white flour for whole-wheat to add fiber + folate
  • Go light on the yellow cheese in the filling — biggest calorie/sat-fat/sodium add
  • Load veg toppings and use the tomato pizza sauce (lycopene) to make it more of a meal
